Why a print comes out the wrong size

The PDF says exactly how large everything is: a 120 mm wide image is 120 mm wide in the file. Between the file and the paper, though, three things can change that.

  • The print dialog scales the page. Browsers and PDF viewers often default to "Fit to page", "Shrink to fit" or "Fit to printable area", which shrinks the whole page by a few percent so nothing lands in the border the printer cannot reach. The same happens when the paper picked in the dialog is not the paper the page was made for, say Letter instead of A4. Choose 100% or "Actual size", and the paper size the page names.
  • The printer driver has a scale of its own. Some drivers keep a "scale to printable area" or a stored zoom, and apply it on top of whatever the dialog says. It hides in the driver's own settings, not in the browser's.
  • The machine itself is not perfect. Paper is pulled through by rollers, and half a percent of slip along the way is normal. Laser printers also heat the sheet, which shrinks it a little. This part is small, but it is the same on every print from that printer, which is exactly what a calibration can cancel.

What the rulers tell you

Every page carries two 100 mm rulers: one along the bottom, one up the left edge. If both measure 100 mm on paper, the image is the size you asked for. If they do not, first look for the scaling in the print dialog and the driver; a ruler that is 5 mm or more off is almost always a setting. If every print from the same printer is off by the same small amount, calibrate. The two rulers are separate because printers rarely err the same way in both directions: the rollers stretch or slip along the direction the paper travels, while the width is set by the print head and is usually closer to true.

Calibrating a printer

  1. Under Printer, leave the calibration on None and print any page at 100%.
  2. Measure both rulers from the 0 to the 100 mark. A tenth of a millimetre matters here.
  3. Click Calibrate…, name the printer, say that the page was printed with no calibration, and type what the bottom and the left ruler measured.
  4. From then on, with that printer chosen, the page is drawn 100 ÷ measured times its real size, across and down separately. A printer that made the bottom ruler 98 mm and the left one 99 mm gets a page drawn at ×1.020 across and ×1.010 down, and its own shrinking brings both rulers back to 100 mm. Print once more and measure to confirm.
  5. If the ruler is still slightly off, calibrate again: under The page was printed with choose the printer, and the new measurement is stacked on the old correction instead of replacing it.

A calibration belongs to a printer and its settings: a different tray, paper or driver may need one of its own, so name them. The rulers take a strip of the sheet, which is why the largest image is a little smaller than the paper; a print that does not fit upright goes on the sheet sideways, and then the two directions swap roles, which the calibration already accounts for.

Where the settings live

Printers and their calibrations are kept in this browser's local storage on this device. The server only ever receives the chosen factor and name along with each preview and PDF, and keeps nothing. Clearing the site's data in the browser removes the printers.
